## Recent

| Version | Date | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| **v90** | 2026-07-31 | **Wrong episode titles on giant shows, fixed at the ladder.** Your One Piece screenshot showed row "1." wearing *episode 62's* title, date and rating — the TMDB canon table can land a single-entry mega-show a whole season off. ani.zip and Kitsu number episodes by the AniList entry itself and can't shift, so the title ladder is now **AniList feed → ani.zip → Kitsu → TMDB (last)**; episode stills/overviews/ratings/runtimes/dates follow the same entry-true order so a row can never mix one episode's title with another episode's date. One Piece now opens with "I'm Luffy! The Man Who's Gonna Be King of the Pirates!" on row 1, 1999 airdate, correct per-episode ratings for all 1171 rows. **One Piece title fix, take two.** The leftover "Episode 62 - …" titles came from the AniList *stream feed* rung of that same ladder: Crunchyroll's One Piece window starts at "Episode 62" and the old offset logic slid it onto row 1. Numbered feeds are now treated as self-addressed (a feed number maps only to its own episode), so rows 1–61 fall through to ani.zip's entry-true titles. **AnimeX hardened + subtitles.** Matching now short-circuits through AnimeX's exact `anime(anilistId:)` handle (one call, no search guesswork — search remains as fallback); verified live that AoT Final Season E1 carries an English subtitle track on AnimeX's sora backend, which the player registers on episode load. **Attack on Titan plays past Season 3 on CircleFTP again.** The site files AoT twice: a 2013 mega post whose four season-labeled buckets (25/12/22/30 eps) hold everything, and newer per-sequel re-uploads ("…The Final Season…"). The old pipeline let those re-uploads *hide* the mega post — first-hit search stopped early, and the franchise-rescue only fired when nothing else matched — then dead-ended, since the re-uploads have no "Season 4" bucket. Now series asks consult **every** title variant and the franchise rescue is **additive**, so the mega post always reaches the season-gates (which still decide, per post, what may serve — no wrong links). Final Season, Part 2 and both "Final Chapters" specials all resolve from the mega post's Season-4 bucket. **Cineby revived (it was silently dead).** Their backend retired the `/mbx` aggregate *and* Neon routes, and the v30 port of Cineby's "mvm1" stream cipher had a rotation transcription slip (`217·i mod 32` instead of `7·i mod 32`), so every payload failed its integrity check → **zero Cineby links**. Re-verified the cipher byte-for-byte against the live site code, removed the dead endpoints (the per-server fan-out is now the whole flow — exactly what Cineby's own player falls back to), and kept the currently-sick upstreams that fail quietly. Live result: Yoru/Breach/Killjoy/Omen return real multi-quality HLS + subtitle tracks again (movie + TV proven). **AnimeX "HTTP 2004 on TV"**: non-Mimi backends now get a Referer+Origin superset attached to every link (some sent only Origin and gate deeper in the chain); providers dictating exact headers (sora/kaa.lt) are untouched. owocdn (uwu) refuses every fetch from all regions I can test, even raw curl — flagged honestly, not code-fixable. **TV-only HTTP 2004, root-caused and fixed.** yuki (the MegaPlay backend) played on your phone but never on your TV — because when an older Cloudstream build (the TV app) can't expand a master playlist into per-quality links, our fallback link was dropping the backend's required headers; MegaPlay re-checks its Referer on every fetch, so it alone always died (headerless-friendly Mimi kept playing, masking the mechanism). The fallback now carries full headers on every link type, on every app version. |
